# Glossary of Core Terms
 
This glossary defines the vocabulary used throughout the WindConfig AI Copilot knowledge base. All names, codes and figures are fictional and exist only for demonstration purposes.
 
## Structure terms
 
**Contract.** The top-level record that groups service arrangements for a set of turbines. A contract can have several availability configurations.
 
**Availability Configuration.** A set of rules describing how availability is calculated for a given contract over a given date range. Each configuration is identified by a GUID, a globally unique identifier generated by the system.
 
**Version.** Every configuration has numbered versions. A version is in exactly one of three states: *draft*, *published* or *archived*. Only one published version may exist per configuration.
 
**Draft.** A working version that may be incomplete. Drafts can be deleted.
 
**Published.** A complete, validated version that is in force. A published configuration cannot be deleted.
 
**Archived.** A version kept for history that is no longer the current one.
 
**Readiness check.** The backend validation that decides whether a version may be published. The AI Copilot never makes this decision; it only explains the rules.
 
## Calculation terms
 
**Calculation type.** The family of availability measure: TBA (Time-Based Availability), YBA (Yield-Based Availability) or RBA (Revenue-Based Availability).
 
**Formula.** The specific expression used to compute availability. A formula must match the configuration's calculation type.
 
**Period hours.** The total number of hours in the evaluated period.
 
**Excluded time.** Time that is removed from the calculation, for example because a variant says it should not count.
 
**Potential yield / Actual yield / Lost yield.** The energy a turbine could have produced, the energy it did produce, and the energy that was not produced because of unavailability.
 
**Potential revenue / Actual revenue / Lost revenue.** The yield terms expressed in currency units.
 
## Wind terms
 
**Low wind variant.** A setting that decides how periods of very low wind are treated: LW_EXCLUDE_BELOW_CUT_IN, LW_COUNT_AS_AVAILABLE or LW_PRO_RATA_POWER_CURVE.
 
**High wind variant.** A setting that decides how periods of very high wind are treated: HW_EXCLUDE_ABOVE_CUT_OUT, HW_COUNT_AS_UNAVAILABLE or HW_STORM_GRACE_WINDOW.
 
## Alarm terms
 
**Alarm category.** A group of alarms. GRID (Grid interruption) and MAINT (Planned maintenance) are required; WX (Weather event) is optional.
 
**Alarm.** An individual event type inside a category, such as GRID_LOSS or SERVICE.
 
## Other terms
 
**Turbine code.** A contract-specific label from T01 to T10. The same code in two different contracts refers to two different turbines.
 
**Park availability.** A numeric value on the configuration that must lie between 0 and 100.

# Calculation Type TBA - Time-Based Availability
 
## Purpose
 
TBA stands for Time-Based Availability. It measures availability as a share of time. The question it answers is: during the evaluated period, for what fraction of the hours were the selected turbines available to operate?
 
Because it counts hours rather than energy or money, TBA is the simplest of the three calculation types. It does not depend on how much wind there was or how much the energy was worth. It only depends on how long turbines were available and which periods are excluded.
 
## Formulas
 
A configuration whose calculation type is TBA may use one of two formulas:
 
- **TBA_STD**: Available hours / Period hours.
- **TBA_EXCL**: (Period - Excluded - Unavailable) / (Period - Excluded).
 
TBA_STD treats the whole period as the denominator. TBA_EXCL first removes excluded time from both the numerator and the denominator, so excluded time neither helps nor hurts the result. A worked numeric example of both formulas is in the document *Formulas for TBA* (doc_id: formula-tba).
 
## Matching rule
 
A formula must match the configuration's calculation type. A TBA configuration cannot use YBA_STD, YBA_LOSS, RBA_STD or RBA_LOSS. Selecting a formula from another family is an invalid combination, and the backend readiness check will not accept it for publishing.
 
## Interaction with wind variants
 
Every configuration must choose one low wind variant and one high wind variant before publishing. For a TBA configuration these variants decide how hours with very low or very high wind are classified. For example:
 
- With LW_EXCLUDE_BELOW_CUT_IN, hours below the cut-in wind speed are treated as excluded time, which is most naturally used together with TBA_EXCL.
- With HW_EXCLUDE_ABOVE_CUT_OUT, hours above the cut-out wind speed are likewise removed from the calculation.
 
The full list of variants is described in the documents on low wind and high wind variants.
 
## Interaction with alarms
 
The alarms included in the configuration determine which kinds of events are recognised. Two alarm categories, GRID and MAINT, are required for publishing, and each needs at least one alarm included. The optional category WX may be added when weather events should be recognised. See the alarm handling policy for details.
 
## When TBA is a natural choice
 
TBA is often chosen when the parties want a transparent, easily audited measure that does not require yield or price data. It is also a common starting point when a contract is first set up, since it needs the fewest inputs.
 
## Draft versus published
 
A TBA draft may be saved without a formula, without turbines or without alarms. Publishing requires completeness, and that decision belongs to the backend readiness check.

# Calculation Type YBA - Yield-Based Availability
 
## Purpose
 
YBA stands for Yield-Based Availability. Instead of counting hours, it compares energy quantities. It answers the question: of the energy the selected turbines could have produced, how much did they actually deliver?
 
Yield-based measures give more weight to windy hours than to calm hours. An hour of downtime during strong wind costs more yield than an hour of downtime in light wind, so YBA reflects the real production consequences of unavailability.
 
## Formulas
 
A configuration whose calculation type is YBA may use one of two formulas:
 
- **YBA_STD**: Actual yield / Potential yield.
- **YBA_LOSS**: 1 - Lost yield / Potential yield.
 
YBA_STD starts from what was delivered. YBA_LOSS starts from what was lost and subtracts the loss ratio from 1. Both produce a value between 0 and 1, which is usually shown as a percentage. A worked example for both is in the document *Formulas for YBA* (doc_id: formula-yba).
 
## Matching rule
 
A formula must match the configuration's calculation type. A YBA configuration cannot use TBA_STD, TBA_EXCL, RBA_STD or RBA_LOSS. If a draft contains a mismatched formula, it can be saved, but it will not pass the backend readiness check.
 
## Inputs
 
YBA relies on yield quantities: potential yield, actual yield and lost yield. The source of these quantities, such as a power curve or measured data, is not described in this knowledge base.
 
## Interaction with wind variants
 
Wind variants are especially relevant for YBA, because wind speed drives potential yield.
 
- LW_PRO_RATA_POWER_CURVE gives credit for low wind periods in proportion to the power curve rather than all or nothing, which suits a yield-based measure.
- HW_STORM_GRACE_WINDOW gives a grace window around storm conditions, so a storm shutdown does not immediately count as lost yield.
 
The other variants are also permitted, as every variant can be combined with every calculation type. The document on low wind variants and the document on high wind variants explain each of them.
 
## Alarm handling
 
The required alarm categories GRID and MAINT must each have at least one alarm included for publishing. The optional WX category, with alarms such as ICE and STORM, can be included when weather-related loss events should be recognised. Alarm assignments are made per turbine and may only reference turbines that are selected in the configuration.
 
## Drafts and publishing
 
Drafts may be incomplete. Publishing requires every rule to be satisfied, including both wind variants being chosen, and that decision is made by backend code, never by the AI Copilot.

# Calculation Type RBA - Revenue-Based Availability
 
## Purpose
 
RBA stands for Revenue-Based Availability. It extends the yield idea by weighting energy with its value. It answers the question: of the revenue the selected turbines could have earned, how much was actually earned?
 
Revenue-based measures reflect the fact that energy is not always worth the same amount at every moment. Production lost during a high-value period counts for more than production lost during a low-value period. In this fictional knowledge base, all revenue figures are given in generic currency units (CU).
 
## Formulas
 
A configuration whose calculation type is RBA may use one of two formulas:
 
- **RBA_STD**: Actual revenue / Potential revenue.
- **RBA_LOSS**: 1 - Lost revenue / Potential revenue.
 
RBA_STD begins with the revenue actually earned. RBA_LOSS begins with the revenue lost and subtracts the loss ratio from 1. A worked numeric example is in the document *Formulas for RBA* (doc_id: formula-rba).
 
## Matching rule
 
A formula must match the configuration's calculation type. An RBA configuration cannot use TBA_STD, TBA_EXCL, YBA_STD or YBA_LOSS. Mismatches can exist in a draft but cannot be published.
 
## Inputs
 
RBA needs revenue quantities: potential revenue, actual revenue and lost revenue. How the price per unit of energy is determined is not described in this knowledge base.
 
## Interaction with wind variants
 
Any low wind variant and any high wind variant may be combined with RBA. Two examples illustrate the contrast in intent:
 
- LW_COUNT_AS_AVAILABLE treats low wind periods as available, which protects the service provider from being penalised for calm conditions.
- HW_COUNT_AS_UNAVAILABLE treats high wind periods as unavailable, which is the more conservative treatment for the service provider.
 
The documents on low wind and high wind variants describe all six variants.
 
## Alarm handling
 
As for every calculation type, the required categories GRID and MAINT each need at least one alarm included when publishing. WX is optional. Assignments are per turbine and may only reference selected turbines.
 
## Publishing
 
An RBA draft can be incomplete. Publishing requires completeness and is decided by the backend readiness check. The AI Copilot may describe the rules, but it never states that a configuration is publishable.

# Formulas for TBA - TBA_STD and TBA_EXCL
 
Two formulas are available for the calculation type TBA (Time-Based Availability). Both produce a ratio between 0 and 1, which is usually shown as a percentage.
 
## TBA_STD
 
**Formula:** Available hours / Period hours.
 
TBA_STD divides the number of hours in which the turbines were available by the full number of hours in the period. No time is removed from the calculation.
 
### Worked example
 
Take a 30-day month, so Period hours = 30 x 24 = 720 hours. Suppose the turbines were available for 684 hours.
 
- TBA_STD = 684 / 720
- 684 / 720 = 0.95
- Result: **95.00%**
 
The 36 hours of unavailability (720 - 684) therefore cost 5.00 percentage points.
 
## TBA_EXCL
 
**Formula:** (Period - Excluded - Unavailable) / (Period - Excluded).
 
TBA_EXCL removes excluded time from the period before computing the ratio. Excluded time is neither counted as available nor as unavailable, which means it cannot raise or lower the result.
 
### Worked example
 
Use the same 720-hour period. Suppose 40 hours are excluded and 32 hours are unavailable.
 
- Numerator: 720 - 40 - 32 = 648
- Denominator: 720 - 40 = 680
- TBA_EXCL = 648 / 680
- 648 / 680 = 0.952941...
- Result: **95.29%** (rounded to two decimals)
 
## Comparing the two
 
Suppose the same 32 unavailable hours were evaluated with TBA_STD and no exclusions. The available hours would be 720 - 32 = 688, and TBA_STD = 688 / 720 = 0.9556, or 95.56%. In that case the figures differ because TBA_EXCL removes the 40 excluded hours from the denominator, while TBA_STD does not. This shows that the choice of formula changes the outcome even when the underlying events are the same, so the formula should be agreed in the contract.
 
## Choosing between them
 
- Use TBA_STD when no time should be removed from the calculation.
- Use TBA_EXCL when certain periods, for example those removed by a wind variant, must be excluded from both numerator and denominator.
 
## Matching rule
 
Both formulas are valid only for a configuration whose calculation type is TBA. Using TBA_STD or TBA_EXCL on a YBA or RBA configuration is invalid. The backend readiness check enforces this at publishing time, and the AI Copilot never decides it.
 
## Rounding
 
The examples round to two decimals for readability. This knowledge base does not specify an official rounding rule.

# Formulas for YBA - YBA_STD and YBA_LOSS
 
Two formulas are available for the calculation type YBA (Yield-Based Availability). Yield is expressed here in megawatt-hours (MWh), and all numbers are fictional.
 
## YBA_STD
 
**Formula:** Actual yield / Potential yield.
 
YBA_STD compares the energy actually produced with the energy that could have been produced.
 
### Worked example
 
Suppose the potential yield for the period is 1,200 MWh and the actual yield is 1,140 MWh.
 
- YBA_STD = 1,140 / 1,200
- 1,140 / 1,200 = 0.95
- Result: **95.00%**
 
## YBA_LOSS
 
**Formula:** 1 - Lost yield / Potential yield.
 
YBA_LOSS focuses on the energy that was lost. The loss ratio is subtracted from 1.
 
### Worked example
 
Suppose the potential yield is 1,250 MWh and the lost yield is 75 MWh.
 
- Loss ratio = 75 / 1,250 = 0.06
- YBA_LOSS = 1 - 0.06
- Result: 0.94, or **94.00%**
 
## Relationship between the two
 
If the potential yield equals the actual yield plus the lost yield, the two formulas give the same answer. Using the figures of the second example, the actual yield would be 1,250 - 75 = 1,175 MWh, and YBA_STD = 1,175 / 1,250 = 0.94, or 94.00%, which matches YBA_LOSS.
 
The formulas can differ when the contract defines lost yield in a way that does not simply equal potential minus actual, for example if some losses are not attributable to the service provider. In such a case the choice of formula matters and must follow the contract wording.
 
## Choosing between them
 
- Use YBA_STD when the actual produced energy is the natural reference.
- Use YBA_LOSS when the contract is phrased in terms of losses that are measured or attributed separately.
 
## Matching rule
 
Both formulas are valid only for a configuration whose calculation type is YBA. Selecting TBA or RBA formulas for a YBA configuration is invalid. A draft can hold the mismatch temporarily, but the backend readiness check will not allow publication.
 
## Sanity range
 
A result should fall between 0% and 100%. A value outside this range indicates inconsistent inputs, for example lost yield larger than potential yield.

# Formulas for RBA - RBA_STD and RBA_LOSS
 
Two formulas are available for the calculation type RBA (Revenue-Based Availability). Revenue is given in generic currency units (CU), and all numbers are fictional.
 
## RBA_STD
 
**Formula:** Actual revenue / Potential revenue.
 
RBA_STD compares the revenue earned with the revenue that could have been earned.
 
### Worked example
 
Suppose the potential revenue is 90,000 CU and the actual revenue is 86,400 CU.
 
- RBA_STD = 86,400 / 90,000
- 86,400 / 90,000 = 0.96
- Result: **96.00%**
 
The shortfall is 90,000 - 86,400 = 3,600 CU, which is 4.00% of the potential revenue.
 
## RBA_LOSS
 
**Formula:** 1 - Lost revenue / Potential revenue.
 
RBA_LOSS subtracts the ratio of lost revenue from 1.
 
### Worked example
 
Suppose the potential revenue is 105,000 CU and the lost revenue is 5,250 CU.
 
- Loss ratio = 5,250 / 105,000 = 0.05
- RBA_LOSS = 1 - 0.05
- Result: 0.95, or **95.00%**
 
## Relationship between the two
 
When potential revenue equals actual revenue plus lost revenue, both formulas agree. In the second example, the actual revenue would be 105,000 - 5,250 = 99,750 CU, and 99,750 / 105,000 = 0.95, which equals the RBA_LOSS result. If part of the shortfall is not counted as lost revenue under the contract, the two formulas can diverge, and the contract wording decides which one applies.
 
## Choosing between them
 
- Use RBA_STD when the revenue actually earned is the natural reference.
- Use RBA_LOSS when losses are tracked and agreed separately.
 
## Matching rule
 
Both formulas are valid only for a configuration whose calculation type is RBA. Using them on a TBA or YBA configuration is invalid. The mismatch can exist in a draft, but the backend readiness check rejects it at publishing time. The AI Copilot explains this rule but does not decide it.
 
## Notes on inputs
 
This knowledge base does not describe how prices are set or where revenue data originates. Questions about these points cannot be answered from this material.

# Low Wind Variants
 
## Why low wind needs a rule
 
When wind speed is very low, turbines may not produce energy even though nothing is wrong with them. Cut-in wind speed is the speed below which a turbine does not generate power. A configuration therefore needs a rule that states how such periods are treated. This rule is called the low wind variant. Both a low wind variant and a high wind variant must be chosen before a configuration can be published.
 
## The three variants
 
### LW_EXCLUDE_BELOW_CUT_IN
 
Periods in which the wind is below the cut-in speed are excluded from the calculation. They are neither counted as available nor as unavailable. This is a natural match with a formula that has an explicit exclusion term, such as TBA_EXCL, although it can be selected with any calculation type.
 
### LW_COUNT_AS_AVAILABLE
 
Periods of low wind are counted as available. The turbines receive full credit for these periods, even though they produce little or no energy. This variant is the most favourable to the service provider.
 
### LW_PRO_RATA_POWER_CURVE
 
Periods of low wind receive partial credit in proportion to the power curve. The lower the possible output according to the power curve, the less the period weighs. This is a middle path between excluding the period and fully crediting it, and it suits yield-based or revenue-based measures.
 
## Summary table
 
| Variant | Treatment of low wind periods |
|---|---|
| LW_EXCLUDE_BELOW_CUT_IN | Removed from the calculation |
| LW_COUNT_AS_AVAILABLE | Counted as available |
| LW_PRO_RATA_POWER_CURVE | Weighted pro rata to the power curve |
 
## Choosing a variant
 
The choice follows the contract wording. In general:
 
- Select LW_EXCLUDE_BELOW_CUT_IN when the contract says that periods below cut-in do not count.
- Select LW_COUNT_AS_AVAILABLE when the contract says that low wind periods count in favour of availability.
- Select LW_PRO_RATA_POWER_CURVE when the contract refers to a power curve and proportional treatment.
 
## Compatibility
 
All three variants can be selected with TBA, YBA and RBA configurations. The knowledge base does not prohibit any combination of low wind variant and high wind variant.
 
## Drafts and publishing
 
A draft may leave the low wind variant unselected. Publishing requires that both wind variants are chosen. Whether a particular configuration satisfies this is decided by the backend readiness check, not by the AI Copilot.
 
## What is not covered
 
This knowledge base does not state numeric cut-in wind speeds or the exact power curve of any turbine, as these are not part of the fictional demo data.

# High Wind Variants
 
## Why high wind needs a rule
 
When wind speed becomes very high, turbines shut down to protect themselves. Cut-out wind speed is the speed above which a turbine stops generating power. A configuration must state how such periods are treated. This is the high wind variant. Both a high wind variant and a low wind variant must be chosen before a configuration can be published.
 
## The three variants
 
### HW_EXCLUDE_ABOVE_CUT_OUT
 
Periods in which the wind is above the cut-out speed are excluded from the calculation. They are neither counted as available nor as unavailable. This variant is neutral toward both parties, since the periods do not influence the result at all.
 
### HW_COUNT_AS_UNAVAILABLE
 
Periods of high wind are counted as unavailable. This is the most conservative treatment for the service provider, because shutdowns caused by high wind reduce the availability result.
 
### HW_STORM_GRACE_WINDOW
 
A grace window is applied around storm conditions. During this window, a storm-related shutdown is not counted against the service provider. The length of the grace window is defined by the contract wording and is not stated in this knowledge base. This variant is related to the optional weather alarm STORM, which belongs to the category WX, but the two are separate settings: the variant concerns the calculation, whereas the alarm concerns which events are recognised.
 
## Summary table
 
| Variant | Treatment of high wind periods |
|---|---|
| HW_EXCLUDE_ABOVE_CUT_OUT | Removed from the calculation |
| HW_COUNT_AS_UNAVAILABLE | Counted as unavailable |
| HW_STORM_GRACE_WINDOW | Storm shutdowns forgiven during a grace window |
 
## Choosing a variant
 
The choice follows the contract wording:
 
- Use HW_EXCLUDE_ABOVE_CUT_OUT when periods above cut-out should not count either way.
- Use HW_COUNT_AS_UNAVAILABLE when high wind shutdowns must reduce availability.
- Use HW_STORM_GRACE_WINDOW when the contract grants a grace window for storms.
 
## Compatibility
 
All three variants can be selected with TBA, YBA and RBA configurations. No combination with a low wind variant is prohibited by this knowledge base.
 
## Drafts and publishing
 
A draft may leave the high wind variant unselected. Publishing requires that both wind variants are chosen. The backend readiness check decides whether a configuration satisfies this rule. The AI Copilot never states that a configuration is ready.
 
## What is not covered
 
This knowledge base gives no numeric cut-out speed and no duration for the grace window.

# Alarm Handling Policy
 
## Categories and alarms
 
Alarms are organised into categories. Each category either must or may be represented in a configuration.
 
| Category | Name | Status | Alarms |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRID | Grid interruption | Required | GRID_LOW (Grid voltage low), GRID_LOSS (Grid loss) |
| MAINT | Planned maintenance | Required | SERVICE (Scheduled service), BLADE (Blade inspection) |
| WX | Weather event | Optional | ICE (Icing), STORM (Storm shutdown) |
 
No other categories or alarm codes exist in this knowledge base.
 
## Required versus optional
 
A category marked *required* must have at least one of its alarms included in the configuration before it can be published. For GRID, including only GRID_LOSS satisfies the rule. For MAINT, including only SERVICE satisfies it.
 
An *optional* category may be left out entirely. A configuration with no WX alarms can still satisfy the alarm rule, because WX does not have to be represented. Including WX alarms is appropriate when the contract recognises weather events.
 
## Per-turbine assignment
 
Alarm inclusion is assigned per turbine. This means that each turbine selected in the configuration has its own set of included alarms. The key constraint is that alarm assignments may only reference turbines that are selected in the configuration. If an assignment names a turbine that is not selected, the configuration violates the rule and cannot be published.
 
Note that turbine codes such as T01 are specific to each contract. An assignment for T01 always refers to the T01 of the contract that owns the configuration.
 
## Drafts
 
Drafts may be incomplete. A draft may have no alarms at all, may include only optional categories, or may contain assignments that are later corrected. These states only become problems when publishing is attempted.
 
## Who decides
 
Whether the alarm requirements are satisfied is decided by backend code in the readiness check. The AI Copilot may explain the policy, give examples and point out which categories are required, but it never states that a configuration is ready to publish.
 
## Examples
 
- A configuration with GRID_LOSS and SERVICE included for all selected turbines satisfies both required categories.
- A configuration with only ICE and STORM included does not satisfy the required categories, because GRID and MAINT have no alarm.
- A configuration with turbines T01 and T02 selected and an alarm assignment for T03 breaks the assignment rule.

# Draft, Publish, Versioning and Deletion
 
## Hierarchy
 
The structure is: Contract, then Availability Configuration, then numbered versions. A contract can have several configurations. Each configuration is identified by a GUID. Each configuration has one or more versions, numbered in sequence.
 
## Version states
 
A version is in one of three states:
 
- **Draft**: a working version that may be incomplete.
- **Published**: a complete and validated version.
- **Archived**: a version kept for history.
 
Only one published version may exist per configuration at any time. The knowledge base does not describe how or when a version becomes archived, and it does not state whether an archived version can be restored.
 
## Drafts may be incomplete
 
Drafts can be saved with missing information. A draft may lack turbines, alarms, a formula or a wind variant. This lets users work step by step without being blocked.
 
## Publishing requires completeness
 
Publishing requires every rule to be satisfied. The rules are decided by backend code and never by AI:
 
1. The name is unique within the contract.
2. The end date is after the start date. Dates may lie outside the contract dates, because contracts renew.
3. At least one turbine is selected.
4. Every required alarm category (GRID and MAINT) has at least one alarm included.
5. Alarm assignments reference only selected turbines.
6. Park availability is between 0 and 100.
7. Both wind variants are chosen.
8. No other published configuration of the same contract shares at least one turbine and also has an overlapping date range (inclusive).
 
In addition, the formula must match the calculation type (TBA, YBA or RBA).
 
## The role of the AI Copilot
 
The AI Copilot explains these rules in plain language. When a user asks whether a configuration can be published, the Copilot describes the relevant rule and states that the backend readiness check makes the decision. It never answers yes or no on its own.
 
## Deletion
 
- **Drafts can be deleted.**
- **Published configurations cannot be deleted.**
 
This knowledge base does not say whether archived versions can be deleted.
 
## Typical workflow
 
1. Create a draft within a contract.
2. Fill in the calculation type, formula, wind variants, turbines, alarms and dates.
3. Run the backend readiness check.
4. If all rules are met, publish the version.
5. To change a published configuration, work on a new version and publish it when ready.
 
Step 5 describes the general intent of versioning. Exact mechanics are not specified here.

# The Overlap Rule
 
## Statement of the rule
 
When a configuration is published, the backend readiness check verifies that no other **published** configuration of the **same contract** both:
 
1. shares at least one turbine with it, **and**
2. has an overlapping date range.
 
Date ranges are **inclusive**. A range that ends on a given day overlaps with a range that starts on that same day. A conflict exists only if both conditions are true. If either one is false, the rule is satisfied.
 
Important details:
 
- Only published configurations are compared. Drafts and archived versions are not part of the comparison.
- Only configurations of the same contract are compared. Because turbine codes are contract-specific, T01 in one contract is a different turbine from T01 in another.
- Dates may lie outside the contract dates, since contracts renew.
 
## Setup for the examples
 
Contract Alpha (fictional) already has one published configuration, P1:
 
- Turbines: T01, T02, T03
- Dates: 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31
 
## Example 1 - Overlap (conflict)
 
A new configuration N1 selects turbines T03 and T04, with dates 2025-12-31 to 2026-06-30.
 
- Shared turbine: T03, so condition 1 is true.
- Date check: P1 ends on 2025-12-31 and N1 starts on 2025-12-31. Because ranges are inclusive, they share that day, so condition 2 is true.
- Both conditions are true, so the overlap rule is **violated**.
 
## Example 2 - No date overlap
 
A new configuration N2 selects turbines T01 and T02, with dates 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31.
 
- Shared turbines: T01 and T02, so condition 1 is true.
- Date check: P1 ends on 2025-12-31 and N2 begins on 2026-01-01, one day later. There is no common day, so condition 2 is false.
- Only one condition is true, so the overlap rule is **satisfied**.
 
## Example 3 - No shared turbine
 
A new configuration N3 selects turbines T04 and T05, with dates 2025-03-01 to 2025-09-30.
 
- Shared turbines: none, since P1 uses T01, T02 and T03. Condition 1 is false.
- Date check: the range lies inside P1's range, so condition 2 is true.
- Only one condition is true, so the overlap rule is **satisfied**.
 
## Notes
 
- Examples 2 and 3 show that overlapping dates alone, or shared turbines alone, are not a conflict.
- The overlap rule is one of several publishing rules. A configuration that satisfies it may still fail other rules.
- The AI Copilot explains the rule but never decides it. The backend readiness check makes the decision.

# Fictional Contract Clause Excerpts
 
The following three excerpts are invented for the demo. They are not real contract language, and they show how clause wording maps to WindConfig codes. The parties are called the Operator and the Provider.
 
## Clause 1 - Time-Based Availability Guarantee
 
> 1.1 The Provider shall maintain time-based availability for the turbines listed in the schedule. Availability is calculated as (Period - Excluded - Unavailable) / (Period - Excluded).
> 1.2 Hours in which the wind speed is below cut-in shall not form part of the calculation. Hours in which the wind speed is above cut-out shall not form part of the calculation.
 
**Mapping:** calculation type TBA; formula TBA_EXCL; low wind variant LW_EXCLUDE_BELOW_CUT_IN; high wind variant HW_EXCLUDE_ABOVE_CUT_OUT.
 
## Clause 2 - Yield-Based Measure for the Second Schedule
 
> 2.1 For the turbines in the second schedule, availability is measured as one minus the ratio of lost yield to potential yield.
> 2.2 Periods of low wind shall be credited in proportion to the power curve.
> 2.3 A grace window applies at the onset of storm conditions, during which a storm shutdown shall not be treated as lost yield. The duration of the grace window is set out in Annex C.
 
**Mapping:** calculation type YBA; formula YBA_LOSS; low wind variant LW_PRO_RATA_POWER_CURVE; high wind variant HW_STORM_GRACE_WINDOW. The weather alarm STORM, in the optional category WX, may be included so that storm shutdowns are recognised. Annex C is not part of this knowledge base.
 
## Clause 3 - Recognised Events and Reporting Period
 
> 3.1 The following events shall be recognised: grid loss; scheduled service; blade inspection.
> 3.2 Icing may additionally be recognised where the Operator and the Provider agree in writing.
> 3.3 The reporting period of a configuration may begin before or end after the term of this agreement, so that renewals can be accommodated.
 
**Mapping:**
 
- Grid loss corresponds to GRID_LOSS in the required category GRID.
- Scheduled service and blade inspection correspond to SERVICE and BLADE in the required category MAINT.
- Icing corresponds to ICE in the optional category WX, included only by agreement.
- Clause 3.3 is consistent with the rule that configuration dates may lie outside the contract dates because contracts renew.
 
## Reading clauses with care
 
Clause wording tells the user which codes to select, but the clauses cannot decide whether a configuration is publishable. That decision belongs to the backend readiness check. The excerpts also contain no penalties, thresholds or percentage guarantees, so questions about such values cannot be answered from this knowledge base.

# Turbine Codes and Turbine Selection
 
## Contract-specific codes
 
Turbines are labelled with codes from T01 to T10. These codes are **specific to a contract**. T01 in Contract Alpha and T01 in Contract Beta are two different physical turbines that happen to share a label. A code is therefore meaningful only together with the contract that owns it.
 
This has an important consequence. When the system compares configurations, for example in the overlap rule, it compares turbines only inside the same contract. A shared label across two contracts never counts as a shared turbine.
 
## Selecting turbines
 
Every configuration selects the turbines it covers. Selection rules:
 
- A draft may have no turbines selected.
- Publishing requires at least one turbine.
- All alarm assignments must reference only selected turbines.
- Two published configurations of the same contract that share at least one turbine must not have overlapping date ranges, with inclusive dates.
 
## Examples
 
**Example 1.** Contract Alpha has a configuration covering T01 and T02. A user wants a second configuration for the same contract covering T02 and T03. The two share T02. If both are published, their date ranges must not overlap. If the dates are different and do not overlap, both can coexist.
 
**Example 2.** A configuration in Contract Alpha covers T01 for 2025. A configuration in Contract Beta covers T01 for 2025. Because these are different turbines, no overlap issue arises.
 
**Example 3.** A user selects T01 and T02 but creates an alarm assignment for T05. T05 is not selected, so the assignment violates the rule that alarm assignments reference only selected turbines.
 
## Changing the selection
 
If a turbine is removed from the selection, any alarm assignments that reference it must also be corrected before publishing. The knowledge base does not describe whether the system cleans these up automatically.
 
## Relationship to required alarm categories
 
Alarms are assigned per turbine, and the required categories GRID and MAINT each need at least one alarm included. The precise way these requirements are checked across several turbines is not detailed here. The backend readiness check applies the rules and decides.
 
## Limits
 
The knowledge base lists only the codes T01 to T10. It does not state how many turbines a real park has, which turbine model each code represents, or any technical data about the turbines.

# Frequently Asked Questions
 
**Q: What are the three calculation types?**
TBA (Time-Based Availability), YBA (Yield-Based Availability) and RBA (Revenue-Based Availability).
 
**Q: Which formulas can I use?**
The formula must match the calculation type. TBA allows TBA_STD and TBA_EXCL. YBA allows YBA_STD and YBA_LOSS. RBA allows RBA_STD and RBA_LOSS.
 
**Q: Can I save a draft that is not finished?**
Yes. Drafts may be incomplete. Only publishing requires completeness.
 
**Q: Which alarm categories are mandatory?**
GRID (Grid interruption) and MAINT (Planned maintenance). Each needs at least one alarm included. WX (Weather event) is optional.
 
**Q: Can one contract have several configurations?**
Yes. A contract can have several configurations, each identified by its own GUID. The knowledge base does not state a maximum number.
 
**Q: How many published versions can a configuration have?**
Only one.
 
**Q: Can I delete a configuration?**
Drafts can be deleted. Published configurations cannot be deleted.
 
**Q: Why did the Copilot not tell me whether my configuration is ready?**
Publishing rules are decided by backend code, never by the AI. The Copilot explains the relevant rule and tells you that the backend readiness check decides. This protects you from relying on an unverified answer.
 
**Q: May the configuration dates fall outside the contract dates?**
Yes, because contracts renew. The end date must still be after the start date.
 
**Q: Does the overlap rule apply across contracts?**
No. It applies to published configurations of the same contract that share at least one turbine and have an overlapping date range, with inclusive dates. Turbine codes are contract-specific, so T01 in two contracts means two different turbines.
 
**Q: What range is allowed for park availability?**
0 to 100.
 
**Q: Do I have to choose wind variants?**
Yes, both a low wind variant and a high wind variant must be chosen before publishing. Drafts may leave them open.
 
**Q: Where can I find numeric details such as cut-in speeds, penalty amounts or grace window lengths?**
Not in this knowledge base. These values depend on the actual contract text and are not part of the fictional demo data.
 
**Q: Does the AI Copilot change my configuration?**
This knowledge base does not describe any automatic changes. Its role here is to explain terms, formulas, variants and rules.
